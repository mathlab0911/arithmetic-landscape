The terminology, counted before it was defended

terms_r128 inventories the vocabulary: 387 occurrences of names this programme
coined against 166 of the shared vocabulary of additive number theory, a ratio of
more than two to one, and 22 of 24 coined terms met somewhere before they were
introduced.  That is the complaint, measured.

Three objects turned out to have two names each, which is the same cost paid
twice for nothing:

  deg_A(n) and r_A(n)                    -- removed at r126
  the window series and the window measure
  the coset identity and the coarse-graining identity

Each is now one name.  The second and third were found by this inventory; the
first was found by reading the abstract.

Every live paper now carries a short terminology table: Part I glosses the
vocabulary of the series in eleven rows, Parts II and III add the three and five
terms local to them and point at Part I for the rest.  The table says, for each
term, what it denotes in ordinary language and whether it is scaffolding or the
point -- for the record, two of the eleven are the point and the rest are
vocabulary for stating them.

C17 keeps the tables complete: a coined term used in a live paper must be glossed
in a table the reader can find.  paper3.tex is excluded, superseded and labelled
as such.

C17's own first negative control passed, which meant the check could not fail.
It searched for the gloss in a "terminology table" region running to the next
\section, so the region swallowed the rest of the introduction -- including
Theorem D, whose name is Sandwich -- and deleting the actual table row changed
nothing.  Narrowing the region to the next sectioning command of any level made
the control fire, and the now-honest check immediately found three real gaps:
"window identity" and "window measure" were used and glossed nowhere.

When a check searches a region, the region is part of the check.  Drawn too wide
it is the same failure as a scope drawn too narrow, and only the negative control
tells either of them apart from a pass.
