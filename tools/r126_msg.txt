Dirk Zeindler declined the endorsement today.  Not on the mathematics -- he says
twice that this is not a judgement of it and that there may well be good work
here -- but because the papers make extensive use of non-standard terminology
and he could not assess them without more time than an endorsement warrants.
He also wrote that he suspects AI tools were used and that this has to be
acknowledged.

He is right about the second point and it was entirely ours.  The disclosure
existed, prominently, in the README, under a heading written so a reader would
meet it.  It appeared in NONE of the four papers.  He read PDFs.  That is the
fourth instance in two days of one shape -- the checks that did not read the
Japanese editions, the check that did not read the README, the README row labels
that switched a page check off silently -- and the first that cost anything.  A
statement that lives one artefact away from its reader has not been made.

Fixed in the artefact the reader holds: every paper now carries a "Use of AI
tools" section saying what the models did, that the author directs and is
responsible, and why the verification apparatus exists.  C16 enforces it, with a
negative control.

The first point is fair too, and the diagnosis is sharper than "the writing is
poor".  An earlier technical review engaged with this same material in detail --
Bikelis, Berry-Esseen constants, splitting the error domain -- so the work is
not unintelligible once you are inside it.  There was no way in.

Two concrete faults, both now repaired in Part I:

- The paper carried two symbols for one object.  deg_A(n), a physics name for
  the ground-state count, is literally r_A(n), the representation count -- and
  the same paper already wrote r_{B_d}(m) for exactly that object on a
  truncation.  \deg is gone from all four papers and both editions; a
  representation count now always carries the same symbol.
- The abstract opened by defining our own invariant, before naming a single
  object the reader already had.  It now opens with r_A(n) and states the main
  theorem with no landscape vocabulary at all:

      [ r_A(n) + sum_d ( r_{B_d}(n+d) + r_{B_d}(n-d-sigma(I_d)) ) ] / r_A(n)
          ->  Gamma(A),

  a weighted count of representations by the truncations of A, over the
  representation count of A, converging to a number read off A by counting.
  door_r126 verifies the identity on 176 cases.  The landscape reading follows
  as the source of the question, and a reader who wants none of it is told so
  explicitly.

Coined vocabulary is a cost paid by every reader and a convenience for the
author.  Before inventing a name, check whether the object already has one.
