The first referee pass, and the three things it caught about our own evidence

Thirteen units through the procedure written yesterday: the statements changed by the lift,
read in a fresh context by a reader given the three jobs and nothing else. Three came back
clear. Ten carried flags, three of which the reader insisted on, and every one of the three is
a claim about our own verification rather than about the mathematics.

One. "The second, independent reading" appeared in four places, "in two parts" in a fifth, and
"took two of them" in a sixth -- three incompatible descriptions of one event, in one
document. And worse than the inconsistency: r162 read the three lemmas, r171 read the RESTATED
proposition, so no single reading had ever covered the appendix in its present form. Now one
formula everywhere -- an independent reading delivered in three parts, each covering the text
as it then stood: the lemmas (r162), the repairs landed against them (r164), the restated
proposition with the T* construction and all five constants (r171). And the appendix head now
says outright that NO SINGLE READING HAS COVERED THE APPENDIX AS A WHOLE, and that a reader who
wants the stronger statement is entitled to know it has not been supplied. referee_pass.md
gains the matching clause: a reading delivered in parts is one reading, and inflating the count
of the expensive instrument is the same defect as substituting the cheap one for it.

Two. "This statement is conditional on nothing" sat in the same paper as "One condition is
attached to that sentence." Both true -- the theorem is unconditional as mathematics, its
algorithmic interpretation is not -- but the absolute one lived in the STATUS block, which is
where readers stop, and its qualification lived a section away. That is the adverb defect with
the roles enlarged. Both consumer STATUS blocks now carry the pointer.

Three. "Exactly three statements" was contradicted by our own account of what the algorithmic
reading had to say before the lift, and the README then compressed the three into "three
headline theorems" when one of them is a proposition. The count now says "exactly three
numbered statements -- and, transitively through thm:rigid, one unnumbered sentence", with the
convention stated where the count is made, because the justification beneath it is about damage
and damage does not respect environments. README reads "three headline results ... two theorems
and a proposition".

Also acted on: the two grades ("proved" and "proved on paper") named as one rung, with the
quotation marks explained as paper-versus-Lean rather than a weaker grade; the line-by-line
claim no longer hoisted over material read at another grain; the impossibility in rem:closing
scoped to the source it is proved against; "no amount of work on the landscape will remove it"
replaced by what is actually supported, ending "we have no impossibility proof and claim none";
and in referee_pass.md the aphorism about three jobs relabelled a design assumption with the
observation that would refute it, plus the cost of the word "always" -- when fresh context is
unavailable the pass is not run and is not recorded as run.

The entry that matters is in the ledger. We had been counting our own verification and got the
count wrong in three different ways in one document, and it was not sloppiness: each phrase was
accurate to what its author was looking at, and consistency across them is a property no author
checks because no author reads them together. The strongest finding is one the author could not
have made. A fresh reader is not a more careful version of the author; it is a different
instrument, and it measures something the author has no access to.
