The instrument round: int64 exact enumeration to k = 46, and a verdict of "consistent"

fable-5's r188 Ruling D found the way past the gap r187 named, by noticing what sparsity means:
in the window the representation counts themselves are small, so exact arithmetic needs no big
integers and the binding constraint is table memory rather than integer size. Overflow is ruled
out by a bound asserted in code -- every table entry counts subsets of at most k elements, so
every entry is at most 2^k, and int64 holds 2^63.

The validation rung came first, as instructed, and it earned its place: ten values of k, the new
instrument reproducing the big-integer reference as identical integers, not close ones. Nothing
downstream would have been believed had it failed.

Reached k = 46 against the previous 28. The registered C-2 criterion opened: ten points with
e/Gamma < 0.1 spanning a factor 87 in e, against the required six and factor two. The relative
error falls to 2.4e-4 by k = 40.

The verdict is a third kind of answer. The decay rate of |lm/r - Gamma| measures 0.2574 +- 0.1319
against the predicted log(2/c) = 0.3567 -- a separation of 0.75 standard uncertainties. The same
statement as a drift: e*(2/c)^k drifts by +0.0993 +- 0.1319 per unit k, consistent with zero,
total drift a factor 6.0 against a scatter of 22.1. So the layer law is CONSISTENT with the
measurement and is NOT confirmed to any precision worth quoting; a wrong law within a factor two
would not have been distinguished. Saying what a measurement could not have distinguished is
part of reporting what it did.

And a reporting defect caught inside the round. The first script ended with "bounded over the
range means the layer law survives this round" -- a definition that a reader takes as a verdict,
with the script never saying whether the quantity is bounded. A conditional whose antecedent is
never evaluated is an assertion with deniability. Fixed by computing the drift and printing it
with its uncertainty instead of asserting the criterion.

The resolution gained is itself the reportable quantity: r187 said "no rate measurable", r189
says 0.2574 +- 0.1319. An experiment that moves a bound is a result even when it does not move a
verdict, and the honest headline is the uncertainty rather than the central value.
