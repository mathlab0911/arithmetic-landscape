Ledger gains two entries. The first: "all rights reserved pending publication" was a
conditional written into a permanent file, and it went on describing a past while the future it
named became the present -- the same defect as a hardcoded commit message that says "for the
first time" on its second run. Anything that says "pending X" needs a person who will notice
when X happens. Alongside it, the scope rule that came out of settling it: a general
delegation of authority did not extend to granting an irrevocable worldwide licence, and the
scope of an authorisation is bounded by what the person could plausibly have been imagining
when they gave it.

The second: a DOI certifies that a version exists and will not change, and to almost any reader
it looks like it certifies more. The caveat therefore travels with the badge -- Zenodo
description under its own heading, release notes, and in bold directly under the README badge,
because the badge is what people copy. Put the caveat where the claim gets copied.
