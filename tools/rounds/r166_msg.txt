Leiden Declaration: ORCID, the disclosure section renamed and extended, and one provision we fail

The author now has an ORCID iD (0009-0000-0890-4395), required to sign the Leiden Declaration
on Artificial Intelligence and Mathematics (June 2026, DOI:10.5281/zenodo.20302944). It is in
the author footnote of all six papers, English and Japanese.

The disclosure section is renamed to the name the Declaration's individual recommendation 01
actually specifies -- "Tool and computational resource disclosure" -- and extended from tools
alone to tools AND computational resources, in all six papers:

  Tools. Claude opus-5 and fable-5; Lean 4 toolchain leanprover/lean4:v4.32.2 with Mathlib;
  lean4checker for the kernel replay; XeLaTeX; Python 3 with nothing beyond the standard
  library.

  Computational resources. One personal computer. No cluster, no accelerator, no compute
  grant. The longest single computation in Part III is a three-minute kernel replay of the
  whole Lean development.

  Responsibility and attribution, per recommendations 04, 05 and 06, including the standing
  commitment to say so in the text where attribution is not possible.

And recommendation 10, which asks whether smaller, non-proprietary, less energy-intensive
systems would suffice, is answered with the half we fail: the verification and the experiments
are small enough for anyone's laptop, the language models are commercial, proprietary and
energy-intensive, and no smaller system was found adequate for that role. Compliance with the
provisions you meet quietly implies compliance with the ones you do not, so the provision we
fail is named by number in the same section.

C16 went red on the rename, and its second clause is why this commit is not a silent
regression: it reported not only that six papers lacked the section, but that it had examined
zero papers carrying the disclosure -- "the check cannot find its subject, which is a failure
of the check and not a pass for the artefact." A naive C16 would have iterated over an empty
set and passed on the exact commit that removed what it protects. It now accepts four names,
old and new, in both languages, rather than swapping one string for another: a check that
recognises only the current wording cannot audit the past, and copies already in circulation
carry the old name.
