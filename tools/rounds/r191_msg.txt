Ruling 3 executed, and doing it showed the split was not the one either of us had written.
Gamma^(q) is a Part III object, so the deformed identity cannot live in Part I. What belongs
there is the sentence underneath it: Gamma(A) = 1 + 2 G_A(1) with G_A(z) = sum_j m_j 2^{-j} z^j,
i.e. Gamma is the value at one point of a generating function -- with the two consequences that
follow at once (R >= 1 is exactly "Gamma is finite", strictly weaker than R > 1; and
G = z/(2-z), R = 2 for the odd numbers). Part III now cites it by \Xref and states only its own
new content: the deformation reads the same generating function at two points instead of one.
cor:oddsclosed is a corollary of it where it stands. The right home for a result is where its
smallest true statement lives, not where it was discovered.

The literature pass, run before any proof attempt as required, returned a half. Transported:
Jentzsch's theorem (1914) -- every point of the circle of convergence is a limit point of the
zeros of the sections -- with Szego's angular refinement and extensions to Dirichlet, Kapteyn and
Neumann series, analytic curves and ultrametric fields. Applied to G, when R = 1 the point z = 1
is on the circle and z = 2q sends it to q = 1/2, so the accumulation of the zeros of each section
at the fair coin is classical. Not transported: our object is 1 + G_k(2q) + G_k(2-2q), two
sections under affine substitutions plus a constant, and adding functions can cancel zeros. No
source covering sums of sections was found. The missing ingredient is now one sentence and it is
in prob:converse at the statement.

And the part that was not expected. Sections of a single geometric series have zeros at angular
spacing 2pi/k; ours sit at (2n+1)pi/2k -- spacing pi/k, offset by half. That is what cos(k theta)
does and what e^{ik theta} does not, so the half-integer ladder is precisely the part the
single-section theory does not describe. The number that did not fit the classical picture was
the one pointing at what was new; it had been in the log for four rounds labelled as a rate, and
the literature pass is what turned it into a signature.

A half-transport is the most useful of the three outcomes, because it converts a conjecture into
a named gap: we now know which sentence is missing and that it is about power series rather than
about landscapes.
