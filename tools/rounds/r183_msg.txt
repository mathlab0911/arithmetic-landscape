With w_j = m_j 2^{-j} and G(z) = sum_j w_j z^j, the identity Gamma^(q) = 1 + G(2q) + G(2-2q)
holds trivially, and three things follow. G(1) = (Gamma-1)/2, so the radius of convergence R
being at least 1 is exactly the statement that Gamma is finite. The limit is analytic precisely
on the lens |q| < R/2 and |1-q| < R/2. And the fair coin is interior if and only if R > 1, at
distance R/2 - 1/2 from the boundary.

For the odd numbers G(z) = z/(2-z), so this IS cor:oddsclosed, R = 2, and the distance is 1/2 --
which is where the zeros were measured to stop. For a_i = 2^i+1, w_j = 1/2, R = 1, distance 0:
the fair coin is on the boundary of analyticity. The two families are the two ends of one scale.

Layer gaps 2c^j give R = 2/c and the prediction dist -> 1/c - 1/2. Measured at seven values of c
at k = 70, the ratio measured/predicted runs 1.0078, 1.0105, 1.0172, 1.0299, 1.0719, 1.2733 --
approaching 1 from above and degrading exactly where a finite k must, namely where the predicted
quantity goes to zero. The 1.27 row is printed rather than trimmed: a table that stops before the
agreement degrades is a table chosen after the fact. At c = 2 the prediction is 0 and k*dist runs
1.5529 to 1.5631 at k = 24..88, rising toward pi/2, the first rung of k arctan 2t = (2n+1)pi/2 --
n = 0 here and n = 1 for a_i = 2^i+1, where the same measurement gave 3pi/2. At the boundary the
rate is (2n+1)pi/2k; the integer depends on the profile, the vanishing does not.
